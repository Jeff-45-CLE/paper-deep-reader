# Evaluation Rubric

Total: 100 points.

## 1. Source Fidelity — 20

- 18–20: Claims, numbers, symbols, and protocol details match the source. Added interpretation is labeled.
- 14–17: Minor omissions or imprecise anchors.
- 8–13: Several unsupported details or mixed source status.
- 0–7: Fabricated or materially incorrect content.

## 2. Research Logic — 10

- 9–10: Scenario, task, limitation, motivation, method, and result form a complete chain.
- 7–8: One link is under-explained.
- 4–6: Motivation or problem boundary remains vague.
- 0–3: The note provides only a topic-level summary.

## 3. Terminology and Notation — 15

- 13–15: Key terms and symbols are preserved, defined, and connected to the method.
- 10–12: Most items are correct, with a few missing dimensions or roles.
- 5–9: Glossary is incomplete or detached from the pipeline.
- 0–4: Original terminology or notation is lost.

## 4. Closed-Loop Method Reconstruction — 20

- 18–20: Every stage has input, operation, output, purpose, and next consumer. Training and inference are separated.
- 14–17: The main flow is complete, with minor missing transitions.
- 8–13: Modules are listed, but information flow is incomplete.
- 0–7: The algorithm cannot be executed from the explanation.

## 5. Equations and Worked Example — 10

- 9–10: Central equations are explained in context, and the example reaches a final output.
- 7–8: Correct explanation with limited intermediate detail.
- 4–6: Equation or example is partially disconnected from the method.
- 0–3: Missing, inaccurate, or misleading.

## 6. Experiment Reconstruction — 15

- 13–15: RQs, protocol, baselines, metrics, settings, results, and ablations are complete.
- 10–12: Main experiments are clear, with a few missing settings.
- 5–9: Results are summarized without protocol or research questions.
- 0–4: Experimental claims are unreliable.

## 7. Claim-Evidence Map — 5

- 5: All major claims have direct evidence and anchors.
- 3–4: Most claims are mapped.
- 1–2: Mapping is incomplete.
- 0: No mapping.

## 8. Language and Feishu Readability — 5

- 5: Direct language, compact paragraphs, clean hierarchy, no prohibited templates.
- 3–4: Minor repetition or formatting issues.
- 1–2: Mechanical prose, long sentences, or excessive tables.
- 0: Difficult to read or dominated by generic AI phrasing.

## Suggested interpretation

- 90–100: Ready for publication as an example output.
- 80–89: Usable after small revisions.
- 70–79: Main content exists, but important gaps remain.
- Below 70: Re-run the deep-reading workflow.
